## 1. Factor Analysis

### 1.1 Observations and Model

There are $n$ individuals and $p$ observed variables. Let the observation vector for individual $i$ be

$$
\boldsymbol{x}_i = (x_{i1}, \ldots, x_{ip})^\top \in \mathbb{R}^p, \qquad i = 1, \ldots, n.
$$

Factor analysis assumes that $\boldsymbol{x}_i$ is generated by a linear combination of $K \ll p$ latent factors (factor scores):

$$
\boldsymbol{x}_i = \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{\eta}_i + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_i, \qquad i = 1, \ldots, n,
$$

where

- $\boldsymbol{\eta}_i \in \mathbb{R}^K$: factor score of individual $i$, $\boldsymbol{\eta}_i \sim \mathcal{N}_K(\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{I}_K)$;
- $\boldsymbol{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times K}$: factor loading matrix;
- $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_i \in \mathbb{R}^p$: idiosyncratic errors,
  $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_i \sim \mathcal{N}_p(\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{\Omega})$,
  $\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_1^2, \ldots, \sigma_p^2)$;
- factors and errors are mutually independent;
- the true latent dimension is typically unknown. Throughout the algorithm below, $K$ denotes the chosen truncation level (sometimes written $K_{\max}$ elsewhere); it should be no smaller than the number of active factors. The MGP prior shrinks redundant columns toward zero rather than setting them exactly equal to zero.

The data are stored in the $n \times p$ matrix $\boldsymbol{X} = (\boldsymbol{x}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{x}_n)^{\top} = \{\boldsymbol{x}_i\}$, with $\boldsymbol{X}_{\cdot j}$ the $j$-th column. Throughout, the variables are assumed centered (the model has no intercept) and the data are assumed complete (no missing entries). In practice, the columns of $\boldsymbol{X}$ should also be standardized to unit sample variance: the MGP prior on the loadings has a fixed unit scale and the prior on $\sigma_j^2$ (with $b_\sigma=0.3$) is calibrated for standardized variables.

### 1.2 Covariance Structure

Conditional on the loadings, the model implies the following covariance matrix for the observations:

$$
\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = \mathrm{Cov}(\boldsymbol{x}_i) = \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{B}^\top + \boldsymbol{\Omega}.
$$

Because $\boldsymbol{\Omega}$ is diagonal, the pairwise covariances (off-diagonal entries) are determined entirely by the loadings:

$$
\mathrm{Cov}(x_{ij}, x_{iq}) = \sum_{k=1}^K B_{jk} B_{qk}, \qquad j \neq q.
$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Dimension |
| --- | --- | --- |

## 2. Prior Setting

### 2.1 Priors 

#### (1) Multiplicative gamma process shrinkage prior on the loadings

For every element $B_{jk}$ of $\boldsymbol{B}$ ($j = 1,\ldots,p$, $k \ge 1$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{jk} \mid \omega_{jk}, T_k &\sim \mathcal{N}\left(0,\; \omega_{jk}^{-1} T_k^{-1}\right), \\
\omega_{jk} &\sim \Gamma\left(\frac{\nu}{2}, \frac{\nu}{2}\right), \\
T_k &= \prod_{\ell=1}^{k} \delta_\ell, \\
\delta_1 &\sim \Gamma(a_1, 1), \\
\delta_\ell &\sim \Gamma(a_2, 1), \qquad \ell \ge 2,
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\{\delta_\ell\}_{\ell \ge 1}$ are mutually independent. Note that $\mathbb{E}[\omega_{jk}] = 1$: the local factor is a mean-one precision multiplier that lets individual loadings escape the global shrinkage rather than systematically rescaling it. Marginalizing over $\omega_{jk}$ given $T_k$ multiplies the conditional variance by $\mathbb{E}[\omega_{jk}^{-1}]=\nu/(\nu-2)$ (finite for $\nu>2$), so the mean-one statement concerns the conditional/typical scale, not the marginal one.

- **Local shrinkage** $\omega_{jk}$: controls an individual loading element and lets isolated large signals escape shrinkage;
- **Global shrinkage** $T_k = \delta_1 \delta_2 \cdots \delta_k$: controls column $k$ as a whole and accumulates with the column index $k$, so that later factors are shrunk more strongly, yielding strong column-wise shrinkage that can be thresholded post hoc for latent-dimension selection;
- Consequently, the marginal prior of $B_{jk}$ is a scale mixture of normals, and $T_k$ has a multiplicative (product) structure, so prior mass concentrates around zero as $k$ grows.

#### (2) Prior on the idiosyncratic error variances

$$
\sigma_j^{-2} \sim \Gamma(a_\sigma, b_\sigma), \qquad j = 1, \ldots, p,
$$

i.e. an inverse-gamma prior on the diagonal entries of $\boldsymbol{\Omega}$ (a standard choice in FA). Here $b_\sigma$ is the Gamma rate and hence the scale parameter of the induced inverse-gamma distribution:
$$
\pi(\sigma_j^2)=\frac{b_\sigma^{a_\sigma}}{\Gamma(a_\sigma)}
(\sigma_j^2)^{-a_\sigma-1}\exp\left(-\frac{b_\sigma}{\sigma_j^2}\right).
$$

#### (3) Joint prior on the loadings, shrinkage and noise parameters

$$
\pi(\boldsymbol{B},\boldsymbol{\Omega},\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\delta})
\propto \left[\prod_{j=1}^p \prod_{k=1}^{K} \left(\omega_{jk}T_k\right)^{1/2} \exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\omega_{jk}T_k B_{jk}^2\right\}\right]
\left[\prod_{j=1}^p \prod_{k=1}^{K} \omega_{jk}^{\frac{\nu}{2}-1}\exp\left\{-\tfrac{\nu}{2}\omega_{jk}\right\}\right]
\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{K} \delta_\ell^{a_\ell-1}\exp\{-\delta_\ell\}\right]
\left[\prod_{j=1}^p \left(\sigma_j^2\right)^{-a_\sigma-1}\exp\left\{-\frac{b_\sigma}{\sigma_j^2}\right\}\right],
$$

where $a_\ell = a_1$ for $\ell=1$ and $a_\ell = a_2$ for $\ell \ge 2$ (with the $\delta_\ell$ block written at truncation $K$). The remaining model block has the fixed prior $p(\boldsymbol{\eta})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}_{K}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_i;\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol{I}_{K})$, which involves no hyperparameter; the full joint prior over $(\boldsymbol{B},\boldsymbol{\Omega},\boldsymbol{\eta},\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\delta})$ is the product of the block displayed above and this factor, and the ELBO includes it through the factor-score divergence (3.3.3).

### 2.2 Truncation and Remarks

1. **Infinite-dimensional truncation:** the prior in (1) formally allows infinitely many factors; in practice the model is truncated to the first $K$ columns, $k = 1,\ldots,K$. Here $K$ is the computational truncation level, not a claim about the true number of active factors. All updates of Section 3.1 are computed at this truncation; $K$ should be chosen no smaller than the number of active factors, and redundant columns can be thresholded post hoc (using a positive threshold: in finite precision a redundant column can collapse to exactly zero).
2. **Why "multiplicative gamma process":** with $T_k = \prod_{\ell=1}^{k} \delta_\ell$, the precision sequence $\{T_k\}$ is built from independent Gamma variables. The expectation $\mathbb E[T_k]=a_1a_2^{k-1}$ increases with $k$ when $a_2>1$; this fact alone does not establish increasing shrinkage. Meanwhile, $\omega_{jk}$ provides element-level adaptive correction.
3. **Practical hyperparameter guidance:** [Durante (2017), Section 3, *A note on the multiplicative gamma process*](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.03408v2) recommends choosing $a_2 \ge 2$ and making $a_2$ moderately higher than $a_1$, while avoiding excessively large values of $a_1$, to facilitate increasing shrinkage across columns. This is practical guidance, not a guarantee of pathwise monotonicity of $T_k$ or stochastic ordering for all parameter choices satisfying these inequalities.

## 3. CAVI Updates for Bayesian FA

### 3.1 Mean-Field Family and Optimal Variational Posteriors

We use the following mean-field family and notation for the optimal factors:

$$
q(\boldsymbol{B},\boldsymbol{\Omega},\boldsymbol{\eta},\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\delta})
= q(\boldsymbol{B})\,q(\boldsymbol{\Omega})\,q(\boldsymbol{\eta})\,q(\boldsymbol{\omega})\,q(\boldsymbol{\delta})
= \prod_{j=1}^{p}q_{j}(\boldsymbol{B}_{j})\prod_{j=1}^{p}q_{j}(\sigma_{j}^{2})\prod_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{p}\prod_{k=1}^{K}q(\omega_{jk})\prod_{\ell=1}^{K}q(\delta_{\ell}),
$$

where each local shrinkage parameter $\omega_{jk}$ and each global increment $\delta_\ell$ gets its own factor. For every factor, the optimal posterior maximizes the ELBO conditionally on the others, $q_m^*(\boldsymbol{\theta}_m)\propto\exp\{\mathbb{E}_{-m}[\log p(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{\theta})]\}$, and stays in the family of its conditionally conjugate prior.

**Notation:**

- $\boldsymbol{M}_{j}$ is the diagonal *expected prior precision* of $\boldsymbol{B}_{j}$, i.e. the expectation of the random precision matrix $\boldsymbol{D}_{j}=\mathrm{diag}(\omega_{j1}T_{1},\ldots,\omega_{jK}T_{K})$ under $q$ (the actual prior precision is the random $\boldsymbol{D}_{j}$; the optimal Gaussian factor uses its mean):
  $$
  \boldsymbol{M}_{j}
  =\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\boldsymbol{D}_{j}\right]
  =\mathrm{diag}\left(\frac{\alpha_{j1}^{\omega}}{\beta_{j1}^{\omega}}\frac{\alpha_{1}^{\delta}}{\beta_{1}^{\delta}},\;\ldots,\;\frac{\alpha_{jK}^{\omega}}{\beta_{jK}^{\omega}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{K}\frac{\alpha_{\ell}^{\delta}}{\beta_{\ell}^{\delta}}\right),
  $$
  i.e. $[\boldsymbol{M}_j]_{kk}=\rho_{jk}\bar m_k^\delta$, where $\rho_{jk}=\alpha_{jk}^\omega/\beta_{jk}^\omega$ and $\bar m_k^\delta=\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\alpha_\ell^\delta/\beta_\ell^\delta$. The precision of $q_{j}(\boldsymbol{B}_{j})$ is $\boldsymbol{V}_{j}^{-1}=\boldsymbol{M}_{j}+\mathbb{E}_{q_{j}(\sigma_{j}^{2})}[\sigma_{j}^{-2}]\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{q_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i})}[\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i}^{\top}]$.
- $\mathbb{E}_{q(\boldsymbol{\eta})}[\boldsymbol{\eta}]=\boldsymbol{w}=(\boldsymbol{w}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{w}_{n})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times K}$ and $\operatorname{Cov}_{q_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i})}[\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i}]=\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{i}=\boldsymbol{\Phi}$ (common across $i$);
- $\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times K}$ collects the loading means; we write $\mu_{jk}:=(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j})_{k}$ for the individual entries.
- $c_{j}:=\mathbb{E}_{q_{j}(\sigma_{j}^{2})}[\sigma_{j}^{-2}]=\alpha_{n}/\beta_{j}$ with $\alpha_{n}=a_{\sigma}+n/2$, and $\boldsymbol{C}=\mathrm{diag}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{p})$ (here $\beta_{j}$ is the scale of the inverse-gamma $q_j(\sigma_j^2)$, i.e. the rate of $\tau_j=\sigma_j^{-2}$; it is also written $\beta_{j}^{\sigma}$);
- $\boldsymbol{A}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}\boldsymbol{V}_{j}$ aggregates the loading uncertainty entering the factor scores;
- for the local shrinkage, $\rho_{jk}:=\mathbb{E}_{q(\omega_{jk})}[\omega_{jk}]=\alpha_{jk}^{\omega}/\beta_{jk}^{\omega}$.

#### Component 1 — factor scores $q_i(\boldsymbol{\eta}_i)$

*Form:*
$$
q_i^*(\boldsymbol{\eta}_i) = \mathcal{N}_{K}\left(\boldsymbol{w}_i,\;\boldsymbol{\Phi}\right).
$$

*Optimal parameters:*
$$
\boldsymbol{\Phi} = \left(\boldsymbol{I}_{K} + \sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j}^{\top} + \boldsymbol{V}_{j}\right)\right)^{-1},
\qquad
\boldsymbol{w}_i = \boldsymbol{\Phi}\,\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\top}\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{x}_i .
$$

Equivalently $\boldsymbol{\Phi}=(\boldsymbol{I}_{K}+\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\top}\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{\mu}+\boldsymbol{A})^{-1}$. The covariance is common to all individuals because $\mathbb{E}_{q(\boldsymbol{B})q(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}[\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\boldsymbol{B}] = \sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j}^{\top} + \boldsymbol{V}_{j}\right)$ does not depend on $i$; sharing across $i$ is a consequence of the derivation, not an assumption, and $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$ itself is *updated at every iteration*.

#### Component 2 — factor loadings $q_j(\boldsymbol{B}_j)$

*Form:*
$$
q_j^*(\boldsymbol{B}_j) = \mathcal{N}_{K}\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_j,\;\boldsymbol{V}_j\right).
$$

*Optimal parameters:*
$$
\boldsymbol{V}_{j}^{-1} = \boldsymbol{M}_{j} + c_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\boldsymbol{w}_{i}\boldsymbol{w}_{i}^{\top} + \boldsymbol{\Phi}\right),
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j} = c_{j}\,\boldsymbol{V}_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_{ij}\,\boldsymbol{w}_{i}.
$$

#### Component 3 — local shrinkage parameters $q(\omega_{jk})$

*Form:*
$$
q^*(\omega_{jk}) = \Gamma\left(\alpha_{jk}^\omega,\;\beta_{jk}^\omega\right).
$$

*Optimal parameters* ($j = 1,\ldots,p$, $k = 1,\ldots,K$):
$$
\alpha_{jk}^\omega = \frac{\nu+1}{2},
\qquad
\beta_{jk}^\omega = \frac{1}{2}\left(\nu + \left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{\alpha_\ell^\delta}{\beta_\ell^\delta}\right)\left(\mu_{jk}^2 + \left[\boldsymbol{V}_j\right]_{k,k}\right)\right),
\qquad
\rho_{jk} = \frac{\alpha_{jk}^\omega}{\beta_{jk}^\omega}.
$$

The shape $\alpha_{jk}^\omega$ is iteration-independent. The rate depends on the second moment $\mathbb{E}_{q_{j}(\boldsymbol{B}_{j})}[B_{jk}^2] = \mu_{jk}^2 + [\boldsymbol{V}_j]_{k,k}$ of the *loadings' own variational factor*: the update is a fixed-point condition, evaluated at the current values of $\boldsymbol{\mu}_j$ and $\boldsymbol{V}_j$, and is only one step of the coordinate-ascent iteration.

#### Component 4 — global shrinkage parameters $q(\delta_\ell)$

*Form:*
$$
q^*(\delta_\ell) = \Gamma\left(\alpha_\ell^\delta,\;\beta_\ell^\delta\right).
$$

*Optimal parameters* ($\ell = 1,\ldots,K$, with $a_\ell = a_1$ for $\ell=1$ and $a_\ell = a_2$ for $\ell \ge 2$):
$$
\alpha_\ell^\delta = a_\ell + \frac{p}{2}\left(K - \ell + 1\right),
\qquad
\beta_\ell^\delta = 1 + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=\ell}^{K}\left(\prod_{\substack{1\le r\le k\\ r\neq \ell}}\frac{\alpha_r^\delta}{\beta_r^\delta}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{p}\rho_{jk}\left(\mu_{jk}^2 + \left[\boldsymbol{V}_j\right]_{k,k}\right).
$$

The shape $\alpha_\ell^\delta$ is iteration-independent; the summation starts at $k=\ell$ because $\delta_\ell$ enters the prior of every column $k \ge \ell$. The rate depends on the same loading second moments, so this update is again a fixed-point condition evaluated at the current values of $q(\boldsymbol{B})$, $q(\boldsymbol{\omega})$ and the other $q(\delta_r)$. Because the rate for $\delta_\ell$ depends on the means of the other $\delta_r$, these $K$ updates are coupled: they should be applied sequentially as coordinate updates, or the coupled system should be solved to convergence. A single parallel update using stale $E[\delta_r]$ is not the joint conditional optimum and need not increase the ELBO.

#### Component 5 — idiosyncratic error variances $q_j(\sigma_j^2)$

$$
q_j^*(\sigma_j^{2}) = \operatorname{IG}\left(\alpha_j^\sigma,\;\beta_j^\sigma\right).
$$

*Optimal parameters:*
$$
\alpha_j^\sigma = \alpha_{n} = a_\sigma + \frac{n}{2},
$$
$$
\beta_j^\sigma \equiv \beta_{j} = b_\sigma + \frac{1}{2}\left(
\left\|\boldsymbol{X}_{\cdot j} - \boldsymbol{w}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j}\right\|^{2}
+ n\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Phi}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j}
+ \operatorname{tr}\left[\boldsymbol{V}_{j}\left(\boldsymbol{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{w}+n\boldsymbol{\Phi}\right)\right]
\right).
$$

The shape $\alpha_j^\sigma$ is iteration-independent. The three terms entering $\beta_j$ are the residual sum of squares and the two uncertainty corrections coming from $\operatorname{Cov}_{q}(\boldsymbol{B}_{j})=\boldsymbol{V}_{j}$ and $\operatorname{Cov}_{q_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i})}[\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i}]=\boldsymbol{\Phi}$. With $c_{j}=\alpha_n/\beta_j$ this is the standard inverse-gamma posterior expressed in terms of the mean precision (equivalently, $\beta_j$ is the Gamma rate of $\tau_j=\sigma_j^{-2}$).

#### Iteration and expectations

All parameters are re-estimated coordinate-wise, with every update using the current values of the other factors; we use the sweep order Component 2 (loadings) $\to$ Component 1 (scores) $\to$ Component 5 (errors) $\to$ Component 3 (local) $\to$ Component 4 (global). The coordinate-wise fixed-point equations are the same for any cyclic order, but the iteration path and, if the iterates converge, the stationary point or coordinate-wise optimum reached may depend on that order. Such convergence does not guarantee that the limit is a local maximum of the full ELBO. Within Component 4, the individual $\delta_\ell$ updates are coupled through the other $\delta$-means and are applied sequentially as coordinate updates (see the note at the end of Component 4). This order is chosen so that Component 5 is the last update among Components 1, 2 and 5, which makes $\beta_j=b_\sigma+R_j/2$ hold with the Gaussian moments current at the end of every sweep (see Section 3.2 for the ELBO evaluation). The component numbers 1–5 are labels, not execution order; one sweep is: (i) Component 2 (loadings), (ii) Component 1 (scores), (iii) Component 5 (errors), (iv) Component 3 (local shrinkage), (v) Component 4 (global shrinkage, with its $K$ coordinate updates applied sequentially). The expectations used are

$$
\mathbb{E}[\omega_{jk}] = \rho_{jk}=\frac{\alpha_{jk}^\omega}{\beta_{jk}^\omega},
\qquad
\mathbb{E}[\delta_\ell] = \frac{\alpha_\ell^\delta}{\beta_\ell^\delta},
\qquad
\mathbb{E}[\sigma_j^{-2}] = c_{j}=\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\beta_{j}},
\qquad
\mathbb{E}_{q_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i})}[\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i}] = \boldsymbol{w}_i,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}_{q_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i})}[\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i}] = \boldsymbol{\Phi},
\qquad
\mathbb{E}_{q_{j}(\boldsymbol{B}_{j})}[B_{jk}^2] = \mu_{jk}^2 + [\boldsymbol{V}_j]_{k,k}.
$$

The shape parameters are iteration-independent. The rates $\beta_{jk}^\omega$, $\beta_\ell^\delta$, $\beta_{j}$, the Gaussian moments $\boldsymbol{w}_i$, $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$, $\boldsymbol{\mu}_j$, $\boldsymbol{V}_j$, and all derived quantities such as $c_j$, $\boldsymbol{M}_j$, $\boldsymbol{A}$, $\rho_{jk}$ and the $\delta$-means change across iterations.

#### Notation summary

| Symbol | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| $\boldsymbol{M}_{j}$ | diagonal expected prior-precision contribution to $\boldsymbol{V}_{j}^{-1}$: $[\boldsymbol{M}_j]_{kk}=\rho_{jk}\bar m^{\delta}_{k}$, i.e. $\operatorname{diag}\big(\rho_{j1}m^{\delta}_{1},\ldots,\rho_{jK}\bar m^{\delta}_{K}\big)$ |
| $c_{j}=\alpha_{n}/\beta_{j}$, $\boldsymbol{C}=\mathrm{diag}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{p})$ | mean idiosyncratic precisions |
| $\alpha_{n}=a_{\sigma}+n/2$, $\beta_{j}$ | shape and scale of $q_{j}(\sigma_{j}^{2})$ (equivalently shape and rate of $q_{j}(\sigma_{j}^{-2})$) |
| $\boldsymbol{w}$, $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$ ($\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{i}=\boldsymbol{\Phi}$) | mean and common covariance of the factor scores |
| $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, $\boldsymbol{V}_{j}$ | mean and covariance of the loading rows |
| $\boldsymbol{A}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}\boldsymbol{V}_{j}$ | aggregate loading uncertainty entering the scores |
| $\rho_{jk}=\mathbb{E}_{q}[\omega_{jk}]$, $m^{\delta}_{\ell}=\mathbb{E}_{q}[\delta_{\ell}]$, $\bar m^{\delta}_{k}=\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}m^{\delta}_{\ell}$ | local and global shrinkage means |
| $a_{1},a_{2},\nu$ | MGP hyperparameters |
| $\omega_{jk}$, $\delta_{\ell}$, $T_{k}=\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\delta_{\ell}$ | local shrinkage, global increments, global shrinkage |

### 3.2 From Coordinate Updates to the ELBO

Each of the five updates is the exact conditional optimum of the ELBO given the other factors (with the coupled $\delta_\ell$ updates applied sequentially, as noted at the end of Component 4), so one full cycle leaves the exact ELBO non-decreasing. With the order of Section 3.1, Component 5 is the last update among Components 1, 2 and 5, and the following Components 3-4 only change $\rho_{jk}$ and $m^\delta_\ell$, which do not enter $R_j=R_j(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{\Phi},\boldsymbol{\mu}_j,\boldsymbol{V}_j)$. Hence
$$
\beta_j=b_\sigma+\frac12R_j
$$
holds with the Gaussian moments current at the end of every sweep, so the compact expression (3.3.7) is exact at every sweep boundary and can be used directly to monitor convergence. Only at intermediate points between coordinate updates (for example when the ELBO is printed after each component) is the general expression (3.3.9) needed; it is exact at any parameter point.

### 3.3 Explicit Form of the ELBO

We evaluate $\mathcal{L}(Q)=\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log p(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{\theta})]-\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log q(\boldsymbol{\theta})]$ in closed form, block by block, for the mean-field family of Section 3.1, where $Q$ denotes the mean-field distribution. Write $\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\boldsymbol{B},\boldsymbol{\Omega},\boldsymbol{\eta},\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\delta})$.

#### 3.3.1 Required moments and expectations

$$
\mathbb{E}_{q(\boldsymbol{B}_{j})}[B_{jk}^{2}] = \mu_{jk}^{2}+[\boldsymbol{V}_{j}]_{k,k},
\qquad
\rho_{jk} := \mathbb{E}_{q(\omega_{jk})}[\omega_{jk}] = \frac{\alpha_{jk}^{\omega}}{\beta_{jk}^{\omega}},
\qquad
m_{\ell}^{\delta} := \mathbb{E}_{q(\delta_{\ell})}[\delta_{\ell}] = \frac{\alpha_{\ell}^{\delta}}{\beta_{\ell}^{\delta}},
\qquad
\bar m_{k}^{\delta} := \prod_{\ell=1}^{k}m_{\ell}^{\delta},
$$

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{q_i(\boldsymbol{\eta}_i)}[\boldsymbol{\eta}_i\boldsymbol{\eta}_i^{\top}]
= \boldsymbol{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{w}+n\boldsymbol{\Phi},
\qquad
\mathbb{E}_{q_i(\boldsymbol{\eta}_i)}[\boldsymbol{\eta}_i\boldsymbol{\eta}_i^{\top}]
= \boldsymbol{w}_{i}\boldsymbol{w}_{i}^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Phi},
$$

$$
\mathbb{E}_{q_{j}(\sigma_{j}^{2})}[\sigma_{j}^{-2}] = c_{j}=\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\beta_{j}},
\qquad
\mathbb{E}_{q_{j}(\sigma_{j}^{2})}[\log\sigma_{j}^{2}] = \log\beta_{j}-\psi(\alpha_{n}),
$$

where $\psi(\cdot)$ is the digamma function and $\alpha_{n}=a_{\sigma}+n/2$. All these expectations depend on the current variational parameters (for example $\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log T_{k}]$ uses the current $\{\beta_{\ell}^{\delta}\}$), so they are re-evaluated at every sweep of the coordinate-ascent iteration. The logarithmic moments under $q$ used below are

$$
\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log\omega_{jk}]=\psi(\alpha_{jk}^{\omega})-\log\beta_{jk}^{\omega},
\qquad
\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log\delta_{\ell}]=\psi(\alpha_{\ell}^{\delta})-\log\beta_{\ell}^{\delta},
\qquad
\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log\sigma_{j}^{-2}]=\psi(\alpha_{n})-\log\beta_{j},
\qquad
\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\log T_{k}\right]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left[\psi(\alpha_{\ell}^{\delta})-\log\beta_{\ell}^{\delta}\right].
$$

For two Gamma distributions in shape-rate parameterization, we use
$$
\operatorname{KL}\big(\Gamma(\alpha,\beta)\,\Vert\,\Gamma(a,b)\big)
=(\alpha-a)\psi(\alpha)
+\log\frac{\Gamma(a)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
+a\log\frac{\beta}{b}
+\alpha\left(\frac{b}{\beta}-1\right).
\tag{3.3.0}
$$

#### 3.3.2 Term 1: expected log-likelihood

Using the bias–variance expectation established in Component 5,
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{q}\big[(x_{ij}-\boldsymbol{B}_{j}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i})^{2}\big]=R_{j},
\qquad
R_{j}=\|\boldsymbol{X}_{\cdot j}-\boldsymbol{w}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j}\|^{2}
+\operatorname{tr}\big[\boldsymbol{V}_{j}(\boldsymbol{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{w}+n\boldsymbol{\Phi})\big]
+n\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Phi}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j},
$$
and the likelihood factorizes over $j$, so
$$
\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log p(\boldsymbol{X}\mid\boldsymbol{B},\boldsymbol{\Omega},\boldsymbol{\eta})]
=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left[-\frac{n}{2}\log(2\pi)-\frac{n}{2}\big(\log\beta_{j}-\psi(\alpha_{n})\big)-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2\beta_{j}}R_{j}\right].
\tag{3.3.1}
$$
The shrinkage prior itself does not appear in this term; it enters only through the posterior moments $R_j$ and $c_j$.

#### 3.3.3 Term 2: noise-variance divergence

Let $\tau_j=\sigma_j^{-2}$. With $q_{j}(\tau_j)=\Gamma(\alpha_{n},\beta_{j})$ and prior $\pi(\tau_j)=\Gamma(a_{\sigma},b_{\sigma})$ (shape-rate parameterization), (3.3.0) gives
$$
\operatorname{KL}\big(q_{j}(\tau_j)\,\Vert\,\pi(\tau_j)\big)
=(\alpha_{n}-a_{\sigma})\psi(\alpha_{n})
+\log\frac{\Gamma(a_{\sigma})}{\Gamma(\alpha_{n})}
+a_{\sigma}\log\frac{\beta_{j}}{b_{\sigma}}
+\alpha_{n}\left(\frac{b_{\sigma}}{\beta_{j}}-1\right).
$$
The transformation $\tau_j=\sigma_j^{-2}$ is invertible, so the same value is $\operatorname{KL}(q_j(\sigma_j^2)\Vert\pi(\sigma_j^2))$. At the Component 5 fixed point, $\beta_{j}=b_{\sigma}+R_j/2$, hence
$$
\alpha_{n}\left(\frac{b_{\sigma}}{\beta_{j}}-1\right)
=-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2\beta_{j}}R_j,
$$
and therefore the Component 5 fixed-point form of the noise KL is
$$
\sum_{j=1}^{p}\operatorname{KL}\big(q_{j}(\sigma_{j}^{2})\,\Vert\,\pi(\sigma_{j}^{2})\big)
=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Big[
(\alpha_{n}-a_{\sigma})\psi(\alpha_{n})
+\log\frac{\Gamma(a_{\sigma})}{\Gamma(\alpha_{n})}
+a_{\sigma}\log\frac{\beta_{j}}{b_{\sigma}}
-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2\beta_{j}}R_j
\Big].
\tag{3.3.2}
$$
As a sanity check, the KL itself is zero when $q_j(\tau_j)=\pi(\tau_j)$, i.e. $\alpha_n=a_\sigma$ and $\beta_j=b_\sigma$. Under the Component 5 fixed-point relation this additionally forces $n=0$ and $R_j=0$, so it is a degenerate consistency check; the unsubstituted KL formula above shows the zero directly.

#### 3.3.4 Term 3: factor-score divergence

Both distributions are Gaussian, so
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{KL}\big(q_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i})\,\Vert\,\pi(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i})\big)
=\frac12\Big(\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{F}^{2}+n\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\Phi}-nK-n\log\det\boldsymbol{\Phi}\Big).
\tag{3.3.3}
$$

#### 3.3.5 Term 4: loading and shrinkage block

The column-$k$ contribution is
$$
S_{k}=\mathbb{E}_{q}\big[\log\pi(\boldsymbol{B}_{\cdot k}\mid\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\cdot k},T_{k})\big]-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\operatorname{KL}\big(q(\omega_{jk})\,\Vert\,\pi(\omega_{jk})\big),
$$
and the global factors contribute $-\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\operatorname{KL}(q(\delta_{\ell})\Vert\pi(\delta_{\ell}))$, so the whole block is $\mathcal{S}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}S_{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\operatorname{KL}(q(\delta_{\ell})\Vert\pi(\delta_{\ell}))$. Since $T_{k}=\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\delta_{\ell}$,
$$
\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log T_{k}]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\Big[\psi(\alpha_{\ell}^{\delta})-\log\beta_{\ell}^{\delta}\Big],
$$
and the loading part evaluates to
$$
\mathbb{E}_{q}\big[\log\pi(\boldsymbol{B}_{\cdot k}\mid\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\cdot k},T_{k})\big]
=-\frac{p}{2}\log(2\pi)+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log\omega_{jk}]+\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log T_{k}]\Big)
-\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{p}\rho_{jk}\bar m_{k}^{\delta}\big(\mu_{jk}^{2}+[\boldsymbol{V}_{j}]_{k,k}\big),
\tag{3.3.4}
$$
with $\bar m_{k}^{\delta}=\mathbb E_q[T_k]$ by independence of the $q(\delta_\ell)$. Writing $\mathrm{KL}(\Gamma(\alpha,\beta)\Vert\Gamma(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}))$ for the Gamma divergence in (3.3.0), the whole block, including the Gaussian normalizing constant, is
$$
\mathcal{S}=-\frac{pK}{2}\log(2\pi)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\Big[\frac12\big(\psi(\alpha_{jk}^{\omega})-\log\beta_{jk}^{\omega}+\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log T_{k}]\big)-\frac12 \rho_{jk}\bar m_{k}^{\delta}\big(\mu_{jk}^{2}+[\boldsymbol{V}_{j}]_{k,k}\big)
-\mathrm{KL}\big(\Gamma(\alpha_{jk}^{\omega},\beta_{jk}^{\omega})\Vert\Gamma(\tfrac{\nu}{2},\tfrac{\nu}{2})\big)\Big]
-\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\mathrm{KL}\big(\Gamma(\alpha_{\ell}^{\delta},\beta_{\ell}^{\delta})\Vert\Gamma(a_{\ell},1)\big).
\tag{3.3.5}
$$
Two remarks on (3.3.4)-(3.3.5). First, the loading-prior contribution inside $\mathcal S$ consists of the explicit normalizing term $-\frac{pK}{2}\log(2\pi)$, the log-precision terms $\frac12\sum_{j,k}\mathbb E_q[\log\omega_{jk}+\log T_k]$, and the quadratic terms $-\frac12\sum_{j,k}\rho_{jk}\bar m_k^\delta e_{jk}$; these are pieces of one expected log-prior, not independent contributions. Second, because the shrinkage factors are ordinary Gamma distributions, the block is fully explicit: all Gamma KL terms follow directly from (3.3.0). At a *fully converged* CAVI fixed point the rates additionally satisfy the conjugacy identities (Section 3.1, Components 3-4)
$$
\beta_{jk}^{\omega}=\frac{\nu}{2}+\frac12\,\bar m_{k}^{\delta}\,e_{jk},
\qquad
e_{jk}:=\mu_{jk}^{2}+[\boldsymbol{V}_{j}]_{k,k},
\qquad
\beta_{\ell}^{\delta}=1+\frac12\sum_{k=\ell}^{K}
\left(\prod_{\substack{1\le r\le k\\ r\neq\ell}}\frac{\alpha_{r}^{\delta}}{\beta_{r}^{\delta}}\right)
\sum_{j=1}^{p}\rho_{jk}e_{jk},
$$
Away from a fixed point these identities do not hold at a sweep boundary: the $\omega$ identity holds only immediately after the Component 3 update (Component 4 subsequently changes $\bar m^\delta$), and the $\delta_\ell$ identity holds only immediately after that single $\delta_\ell$ update (later $\delta$ updates change the products, so at the boundary only the last-updated $\ell$ keeps its identity). They are therefore consistency checks for a converged run, not for every sweep. At the fixed point the first identity is equivalent to $\bar m_k^\delta e_{jk}=2\beta_{jk}^\omega-\nu$; this substitution must not be inserted into the quadratic term of $\mathcal S$ when (3.3.7) is evaluated at a sweep boundary.

#### 3.3.6 Term 5: loading entropy

The Gaussian entropy of $q(\boldsymbol{B})=\prod_{j}q_{j}(\boldsymbol{B}_{j})$ is
$$
-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}_{q_{j}(\boldsymbol{B}_{j})}[\log q_{j}(\boldsymbol{B}_{j})]
=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left[\frac{K}{2}(1+\log 2\pi)+\frac12\log\det\boldsymbol{V}_{j}\right],
\tag{3.3.6}
$$

#### 3.3.7 Assembled ELBO

We collect the five blocks with $e_{jk}:=\mu_{jk}^{2}+[\boldsymbol{V}_{j}]_{k,k}$, $\alpha_{n}=a_{\sigma}+n/2$ and $c_{j}=\alpha_{n}/\beta_{j}$. The key cancellation is worked out explicitly.

**Step 1: log-likelihood minus noise divergence.** At the Component 5 fixed point, use (3.3.1) and the substituted form (3.3.2). The two residual contributions cancel exactly:
$$
-\frac{\alpha_n}{2\beta_j}R_j-\left(-\frac{\alpha_n}{2\beta_j}R_j\right)=0.
$$
The digamma terms also cancel because $\alpha_n-a_\sigma=n/2$, and the logarithms combine as
$$
-\frac n2\log\beta_j-a_\sigma\log\beta_j=-\alpha_n\log\beta_j.
$$
Consequently,
$$
\text{(3.3.1)}-\text{(3.3.2)}
=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Big[
-\alpha_n\log\beta_j
+\log\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_n)}{\Gamma(a_\sigma)}
+a_\sigma\log b_\sigma
-\frac n2\log(2\pi)
\Big].
\tag{3.3.7a}
$$
Thus no explicit $R_j$ term remains after the noise factor has been updated to $\beta_j=b_\sigma+R_j/2$.

**Step 2: the other three blocks.** Term 3 contributes
$$
-\frac12\big(\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_F^2+n\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\Phi}-nK-n\log\det\boldsymbol{\Phi}\big),
$$
Term 5 contributes $\sum_j[\frac K2(1+\log2\pi)+\frac12\log\det\boldsymbol V_j]$, and the shrinkage block, including the loading-prior normalizing constant, is
$$
\mathcal{S}=-\frac{pK}{2}\log(2\pi)
+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Big[
\frac12\big(\psi(\alpha_{jk}^{\omega})-\log\beta_{jk}^{\omega}
+\textstyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}(\psi(\alpha_{\ell}^{\delta})-\log\beta_{\ell}^{\delta})\big)
-\frac12 \rho_{jk}\bar m_{k}^{\delta}e_{jk}
-\mathrm{KL}\big(\Gamma(\alpha_{jk}^{\omega},\beta_{jk}^{\omega})\Vert\Gamma(\tfrac{\nu}{2},\tfrac{\nu}{2})\big)\Big]
-\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\mathrm{KL}\big(\Gamma(\alpha_{\ell}^{\delta},\beta_{\ell}^{\delta})\Vert\Gamma(a_{\ell},1)\big).
\tag{3.3.7b}
$$

**Final closed form at a sweep boundary.** Under the sweep order of Section 3.1, the identity $\beta_j=b_\sigma+R_j/2$ holds at the end of every sweep, so collecting Steps 1-2 and the Gaussian factors ($nK/2$ from the score term and $pK/2(1+\log 2\pi)$ from the loading entropy),
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}(Q)=\;&C_{\mathrm{ELBO}}
-\alpha_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\log\beta_{j}
+\frac{n}{2}\log\det\boldsymbol{\Phi}
-\frac12\big(\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{F}^{2}+n\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\Phi}\big)
+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{p}\log\det\boldsymbol{V}_{j}
+\mathcal{S},
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.3.7}
$$
with the parameter-independent constant
$$
C_{\mathrm{ELBO}}=p\left[\log\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{n})}{\Gamma(a_{\sigma})}+a_{\sigma}\log b_{\sigma}-\frac{n}{2}\log(2\pi)\right]+\frac{pK}{2}(1+\log2\pi)+\frac{nK}{2}.
\tag{3.3.8}
$$
The $-\frac{pK}{2}\log(2\pi)$ normalization of the loading prior is included in $\mathcal S$; it should therefore not be subtracted a second time in $C_{\mathrm{ELBO}}$.

If the ELBO is evaluated at a point where $\beta_j$ is not synchronized with the current Gaussian moments through $\beta_j=b_\sigma+R_j/2$—for example between coordinate updates, or under a different sweep order—the unsimplified noise block adds back the fixed-point correction
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{general}}(Q)
=\mathcal{L}(Q)
+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left[
\alpha_n-c_j\left(b_\sigma+\frac12R_j\right)
\right],
\qquad c_j=\frac{\alpha_n}{\beta_j}.
\tag{3.3.9}
$$
The correction vanishes immediately after a Component 5 update and is generally nonzero after a Component 1 or Component 2 update, since those refresh the Gaussian moments entering $R_j$. Thus (3.3.9) is the exact ELBO at any parameter point, while (3.3.7) is exact at every sweep boundary under the order of Section 3.1.

For reference, (3.3.2) should be read as the fixed-point-substituted form of the noise KL: it is obtained from the exact KL (3.3.10)
$$
\mathrm{KL}_j^{\mathrm{exact}}
=(\alpha_n-a_\sigma)\psi(\alpha_n)
+\log\frac{\Gamma(a_\sigma)}{\Gamma(\alpha_n)}
+a_\sigma\log\frac{\beta_j}{b_\sigma}
+\alpha_n\left(\frac{b_\sigma}{\beta_j}-1\right)
\tag{3.3.10}
$$
by replacing $\alpha_n(b_\sigma/\beta_j-1)$ with $-\alpha_nR_j/(2\beta_j)$ at $\beta_j=b_\sigma+R_j/2$. Subtracting this *unsubstituted* KL from Term 1 gives the general noise block
$$
G_j=\log\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_n)}{\Gamma(a_\sigma)}+a_\sigma\log b_\sigma-\frac n2\log(2\pi)-\alpha_n\log\beta_j+\alpha_n-c_j\left(b_\sigma+\frac12R_j\right),
\qquad c_j=\frac{\alpha_n}{\beta_j},
$$
which reduces to the bracket of (3.3.7a) exactly when $\beta_j=b_\sigma+\frac12R_j$; the extra term is the correction displayed in (3.3.9). A literal Term 1 minus (3.3.2), with (3.3.2) already containing the fixed-point substitution, reproduces the bracket of (3.3.7a) rather than $G_j$.
